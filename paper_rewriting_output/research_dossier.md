## Venue Requirements
For an IEEE-style conference paper, the operative constraint is not just template compliance but contribution density under a short-paper budget. In the absence of a paper-specific CFP, the prior ICCD-style assumption of an 8-page main-paper limit remains a reasonable target, so the manuscript should be planned as a compact full paper rather than an expanded journal article. The paper needs a standard conference arc: problem setup, concise motivating example, method, implementation details only where they support novelty, evaluation, related work, and conclusion. If the submission is to a double-blind venue, the current author block and corresponding-author line in the local draft must be removed for submission; if the venue is not anonymous, they can remain. Figures and algorithms must justify their space cost, because long preliminaries and oversized visual explanations are usually the first source of page pressure.

## Review Criteria
Conference reviewers will primarily evaluate four things: novelty, technical soundness, empirical support, and clarity of claimed scope. For this topic, novelty must be stated narrowly and defensibly: dataflow-guided pruning inside RTL symbolic execution, specifically within combinational logic or always-block-level path exploration, not a general solution to symbolic-execution scalability. Soundness depends on showing why pruning does not remove behaviorally distinct states. Empirical support should show meaningful reduction in explored paths, runtime, or solver load, while preserving bug-finding or reachable-state coverage. Clarity matters because hardware verification reviewers are sensitive to overclaiming; they will look for precise assumptions, boundary conditions, and honest discussion of where the pruning does not apply.

## Accepted Paper Patterns
A strong conference pattern for this kind of systems-and-method paper is: quickly frame the scalability bottleneck, give one concrete RTL example that exposes redundant exploration, define the pruning insight with just enough formalism, then move rapidly to implementation and evidence. A second common pattern is contribution compression: one central mechanism, one careful correctness argument, and one evaluation story built around representative benchmarks rather than a broad survey of loosely connected experiments. Related work is typically compressed and contrastive, used to position the single main idea rather than to build a long background tutorial.

## Constraints for This Paper
The current draft already fits the broad conference shape, but several constraints are immediate. First, the title, abstract, and contribution bullets should consistently emphasize one contribution: dataflow-guided pruning of redundant symbolic paths in combinational RTL. Second, the current framing sometimes drifts between SoC security verification, general RTL symbolic execution, and combinational-logic pruning; that scope needs tightening so the evaluation matches the claims. Third, the draft appears non-anonymous and too expansive in preliminaries and motivation for an 8-page target. Fourth, the evidence base appears limited to two RISC-V cores and one HACK@DAC SoC case, so the paper should avoid broad scalability claims and instead stress targeted improvement over Sylvia on representative designs, with explicit discussion of what is and is not preserved.
